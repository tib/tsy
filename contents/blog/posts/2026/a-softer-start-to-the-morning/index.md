I used to think a good morning had to be highly optimized. I would plan the perfect routine, then feel behind as soon as one part slipped. Lately I have been trying something smaller: sit up, drink some water, and take five slow breaths before I look at a screen.

It is not a dramatic transformation. Some mornings I still reach for my phone too quickly. But when I do make that little pause, I notice the day arriving instead of immediately reacting to it. My shoulders soften. I remember that the first notification does not have to set the pace for everything that follows.

The routine is useful because it is easy to return to. A practice does not need to be impressive to be meaningful. It only needs to make enough room for me to choose what comes next.
