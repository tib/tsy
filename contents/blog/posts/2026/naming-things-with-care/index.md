Naming is one of the smallest decisions I make in code, and one of the easiest to underestimate. A type or function name is a tiny piece of documentation that appears exactly where someone needs it.

I try to name things for the role they play rather than the way I happened to implement them today. That does not mean every name must be long or perfectly descriptive. It means the next person should have a reasonable chance of understanding why the thing exists.

Sometimes I write a name, use it in a few places, and then realize it points in the wrong direction. Renaming it is not wasted work. It is a sign that my understanding has become more precise.

Good names do not remove the need for good structure. They do make a codebase feel more welcoming, and that is worth a few extra moments of care.
