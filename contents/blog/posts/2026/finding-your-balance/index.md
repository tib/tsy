For a long time, my days had a familiar shape: open the editor, solve the next problem, follow the idea until it becomes something real. I love that feeling. Building software asks for curiosity and patience, and it can make a complicated idea feel wonderfully clear.

But a life can become a little too narrow when every question leads back to a screen. I started wondering what it would feel like to learn something with my whole body, not just my hands and eyes. That question brought me to yoga.

## A different kind of practice

Yoga is often described through poses, but the poses are only one part of it. The practice asks you to pay attention: to your breath, to the ground under your feet, to the difference between effort and strain. There is no shortcut to that attention. You return, notice what is here, and begin again.

That rhythm feels surprisingly familiar. Good software rarely comes from rushing at every problem at once. It comes from making space to understand what is happening, choosing a useful next step, and being willing to revise your first idea.

> Progress can be quiet. Sometimes it is simply choosing to show up again.

## Learning to leave space

I am still learning. Some days the balance feels natural; other days I catch myself carrying the pace of work into everything else. The point is not to create a perfect schedule or turn rest into another goal to optimize. It is to notice when I have stopped paying attention, and to make a little room to come back.

For me, that room might be a few minutes on the mat, a walk without a podcast, or closing the laptop when the work can wait. Small choices, repeated with care, can change the texture of a day.

## One step at a time

I want this journal to hold notes from both sides of that life: ideas about Swift and building software, alongside what I learn through movement, teaching, and being a beginner again. The paths do not need to compete. They can make each other more human.

So this is a beginning, not a grand conclusion. If you are also trying to make room for a new practice, I hope you find a pace that feels like your own.
